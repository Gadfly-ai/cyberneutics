## Mistakes and Lessons (for slide authors)

- **Mistake to avoid:** Saying the demo "proves committees are better." The demo **illustrates** a *pipeline* (naive vs. multi-voice rounds vs. rubric evaluation). Outcomes depend on model, prompts, question, and mode (`local` canned vs `api` live).
- **Mistake to avoid:** Calling the demo "the methodology." The methodology is the **repo** (essays, artifacts, skills, deliberation records). The demo is a **thin, runnable slice** aligned with `artifacts/adversarial-committees.md`.
- **Mistake to avoid:** Introducing **palgebra** or **fan/funnel** in the first five minutes to a general audience. That is optional depth for a theory track; the demo UI does not show string diagrams.
- **Lesson:** Teach **narrative engine vs. calculator** *before* showing side-by-side panels; otherwise people think the point is "more text" rather than **staged composition** and **independent evaluation**.

## Theoretical/Conceptual Notes (what the talk must install)

These are the **load-bearing ideas** before the demo makes sense:

1. **LLMs as narrative engines.** A single call produces a **locally plausible story**, not a guaranteed map of the decision space. Tie to README: narrative computing vs. narrative engineering.
2. **Why one voice fails on hard questions.** Centered "reasonable" answers, hidden assumptions, genre defaults — from `artifacts/adversarial-committees.md` opening.
3. **Adversarial committee (operational definition).** Fixed roster, same question, structured rounds, disagreement as **feature**, output as **map of trade-offs and blind spots**, not forced consensus.
4. **Why the roster is fixed.** Dynamic personas let the model manufacture convenient disagreement; fixed personas accumulate meaning across sessions. Source: same artifact, "Why Fixed Characters Matter."
5. **Independent evaluation.** A separate scoring pass (fresh framing / rubric) is **feedback**, not theater — analogous to test vs. train separation in ML, but for deliberation quality signals.
6. **User as editor.** Observation changes state; the human curates what becomes "real" outside the chat. Lightly reference `artifacts/start-here.md` gist if needed.
7. **Demo scope.** The app **instantiates** the naive path and a **committee-shaped** multi-phase path plus rubric UI; it does **not** replace reading `agent/roster.md`, running `/committee` in Cursor, or full scenario→committee→probe lifecycles unless mg adds that narrative.

Optional **advanced** hooks (Workshop / Theorist track): **fan vs. funnel**, **palgebra as enrichment vs. transformation**, pointers to `palgebra/committee-as-palgebra.md` and `essays/10-decisions-under-uncertainty.md` — only after the operational story lands.

## Learning objectives (measurable)

After the talk, a listener should be able to:

- **Explain** why the left panel (naive) and right panel (committee) are **comparable baselines** on the **same question**.
- **Name** the five characters at a high level (or know they represent **non-overlapping epistemic stances**).
- **Describe** what a "phase" or "round" means in the UI (first pass vs. cross-examination / follow-up in API mode; scripted phases in local mode).
- **Interpret** evaluation dimensions at a glance (not memorize names, but understand "quality of reasoning map" not "objective truth score").
- **Choose** `local` vs `api` mode intentionally when following along.
- **Avoid** the two bad conclusions: "bigger prompt = science" and "higher score = always pick committee."

## Standard track — detailed slide-by-slide blueprint

Numbers are suggestions; merge/split for pacing. **Speaker notes** are indented bullets.

### Slide 1 — Title

- **On-slide title:** Cyberneutics — narrative engineering for narrative engines
- **Subtitle:** Hands-on demo: one question, two pipelines
- **Footer:** mg / venue / date
- **Speaker notes:** Frame the session as **method + runnable illustration**, not a product pitch. Point to repo if a link is allowed.

### Slide 2 — Three computing regimes (motivation)

- **Headline:** Numeric → symbolic → **narrative** computing
- **Bullets:** Each regime got an engineering discipline; LLMs are the narrative primitive (`README.md` opening metaphor).
- **Visual:** Simple three-box timeline.
- **Speaker notes:** Avoid over-claiming history; this is **pedagogical framing**.

### Slide 3 — Narrative engine vs. calculator (the category error)

- **Headline:** If the tool is a story generator, "just answer" is the wrong contract
- **Bullets:** Single completion is **one** plausible narrative; usefulness depends on **how** we compose calls.
- **Speaker notes:** Tie to practitioner pain: brilliant prose, wrong problem, hidden assumptions.

### Slide 4 — Cyberneutics in one sentence

- **Use or paraphrase** `artifacts/start-here.md` gist: collaborative sense-making; decisions under genuine uncertainty; explore → evaluate adversarially → trace commitments.
- **Speaker notes:** Emphasize **traceability** and **uncertainty**, not "accuracy hype."

### Slide 5 — The core contrast the demo encodes

- **Headline:** Same question — two generation strategies
- **Two columns:** **Naive** — one shot, one voice. **Committee** — fixed characters, structured rounds, staged disagreement.
- **Speaker notes:** This is the **entire narrative spine** of the UI.

### Slide 6 — Why multi-agent *without* structure fails

- **Headline:** Ad-hoc personas let the model fake diversity
- **Bullets:** Dynamic casting → convenient agreement; **fixed roster** → real tension (`artifacts/adversarial-committees.md` "Why Fixed Characters Matter").
- **Speaker notes:** Connect to **institutional memory** of roles across sessions.

### Slide 7 — The five stances (overview table)

- **Table:** Maya / Frankie / Joe / Vic / Tammy with **propensity** one-liners (from `demo/lib/characters.ts` or artifact table).
- **Speaker notes:** Do **not** read full prompts; say "each is calibrated to catch a class of failure."

### Slide 8 — Phases / rounds (what "Phase 1 / Phase 2" means)

- **Headline:** Staged composition, not a group chat
- **Bullets:** Round one: independent takes (with optional research in API). Round two: **cross-examination** with shared context. Later: follow-ups within configured bounds.
- **Speaker notes:** Local mode uses **scripted** text; still teaches **shape** of the pipeline (`demo/README.md`).

### Slide 9 — Streaming / SSE (optional technical slide)

- **Headline:** Why text appears incrementally
- **One line:** Committee route streams **typed events** (`text/event-stream`).
- **Speaker notes:** Skip for non-engineers; keep for builders.

### Slide 10 — Independent evaluation

- **Headline:** Score the **transcript**, not the vibes
- **Bullets:** Separate call; same rubric shape in local and API modes; dimensions listed next slide.
- **Speaker notes:** This is **feedback in the engineering sense** — highlights weak epistemic coverage.

### Slide 11 — Rubric dimensions (the scorecard)

- **List the five keys in plain English:**
  - Perspective completeness — multiple lenses considered?
  - Tradeoff explicitness — costs and tensions named?
  - Assumption surfacing — hidden premises dragged into light?
  - Evidence standards — claims proportioned to support?
  - Reasoning completeness — steps hang together?
- **On-slide:** Also show tier idea (`STRONG` / `ADEQUATE` / `WEAK`) as coarse banding (`demo/lib/types.ts`).
- **Speaker notes:** Explicitly warn against **reifying** numbers.

### Slide 12 — What you will see in the browser (UI map)

- **Diagram:** Question box → Naive panel | Committee panel → Evaluation section.
- **Bullets:** Mention **About this demo** for glossary; other dashboards as optional depth.
- **Speaker notes:** Permission to ignore extra panels on first visit.

### Slide 13 — Local vs API (honesty slide)

- **Table:** Local — no key, deterministic / heuristic; good for teaching shape. API — live Claude, richer behavior; needs key and caution re cost/latency.
- **Speaker notes:** Align with mg's actual demo plan.

### Slide 14 — Data stays on your machine

- **Headline:** No Cyberneutics cloud in this demo
- **Bullets:** Keys on server env; history in **localStorage** (`demo/README.md`).
- **Speaker notes:** Sets privacy expectations.

### Slide 15 — Live demo transition

- **Large text:** "Now we open the app."
- **Bullets:** Preset question first; narrate **same question** both sides; run evaluation; compare **patterns**, not just scores.
- **Speaker notes:** Use script from §Live demo bridge script.

### Slide 16 — Reading order after the talk

- **Bullets:** `artifacts/start-here.md` → `artifacts/adversarial-committees.md` → `demo/README.md` → optional `palgebra/committee-as-palgebra.md`.
- **QR / link** to repo if allowed.

### Slide 17 — Closing frame

- **Headline:** Compose unreliable narratives into **auditable** reasoning maps
- **Speaker notes:** Invite questions; repeat "user is editor" if time.

## Live demo bridge script (suggested verbatim)

Use a version of this when switching from slides to screen share:

> "Everything I just said is **one runnable slice** in the repo. We're going to type a **single question** into both paths. Watch the **left**: one completion, one voice. Watch the **right**: the same question moving through **fixed roles** and **phases** — the disagreement is intentional. After both finish, we'll hit **evaluate** — not to crown a winner, but to see **where each narrative is strong or thin** on explicit trade-offs and assumptions. If you're following on your laptop, turn on **local mode** if you don't have an API key — you'll still see the **shape** of the pipeline."
